Resolve a locked session

Resolve a locked session

Find out why Kumoss locked your session, who was notified, and how to get it unlocked so you can merge and apply.

Kumoss checks every generate round before it lets you merge and apply. When a check fails, the session is locked: you can keep working on it, but merge and apply are refused until the lock is cleared. This guide explains why a session gets locked, what you see, and the two ways to get it unlocked.

Prerequisites

  • A session whose latest generate round finished, reached by following Follow a session.

  • To unlock a session yourself, the panel role editor or admin; see Roles and permissions. Without it, you need a follow-up request or a reviewer who has it.

Why a session is locked

Three independent checks run at the end of every generate round, and any one of them can lock the session. Only the first is deterministic: the same plan always gets the same verdict. The other two are judgments made by a language model, because they depend on what you asked for and on what a change means, which no fixed rule over the plan can capture. For the mechanism as a whole, see How compliance works. Drift and import rounds are never checked and never lock a session.

All three are prompts in Langfuse, seeded from core/prompts/seed/general/ (and, for the provider policies, from core/prompts/seed/<provider>/). A change takes effect on the next generate round once its new version carries your environment’s label. Which checks run at all is set by your operator with the orchestration switches; see Configuration.

Compliance policy (deterministic)

What locks. Open Policy Agent evaluates your organization’s compliance policies, Rego modules, against the plan’s JSON: the general policy, and one policy for each provider the module declares. Any deny finding locks the session; warn findings are reported but do not.

  • The general policy’s critical_deletion rule denies any resource that is destroyed without being recreated, even when you asked for the deletion. Replacements are allowed.

  • The other shipped rules deny removing a customer-managed key, having Terraform run commands (provisioners, null_resource, external data sources), and provider-specific risks such as public ingress or wildcard admin roles; see The shipped rules.

  • If a policy cannot be evaluated at all, the round gets a policy_unavailable finding and is locked too: the policies fail closed.

Findings from this check carry a policy badge.

Why it is deterministic. No model is involved: the rules are code that reads resource types, actions, and attribute values from the plan. The same plan always gets the same verdict, and every rule can be tested with opa test before it is rolled out.

Where to change it. The general policy is the Langfuse prompt general/policies/compliance, and each provider’s is <provider>/policies/compliance, seeded from core/prompts/seed/<scope>/policies/compliance.rego. To add a rule, such as allowed regions, no public storage, or no owner role assignments, write it in the policy of the provider it concerns (or in the general policy when it spans providers), test it, and publish it as a new version in Langfuse; see Write a policy rule. The input a policy receives and the decision it must return are in Compliance policy.

Use the policies for every rule you can state as a condition on the plan: they are the only check that cannot miss a case or flag one by mistake.

Intent check (not deterministic)

What locks. A language-model agent judges only whether the plan does what the requests of the session ask, every request and not just the first. It reports violations with a rule id and a severity; a finding of severity error or critical locks the session, and warning is reported only. The shipped rules, both of severity error, are:

  • scope_exceeded: the plan changes something you did not ask for, for example it removes a block or sets an attribute to null. A removal is never accepted as a side effect of an addition.

  • scope_incomplete: part of your request has no matching change in the plan.

The intent check does not judge safety, conventions, deletions, or versions, and a change you asked for is never an intent violation. Your organization’s conventions only guide generation: nothing checks a plan against them afterwards except the rules of the compliance policy.

Findings from this check carry an intent badge.

Why it is not deterministic. Whether a change is "what you asked for" depends on the meaning of your request, written in natural language; there is no attribute in the plan to test. A model can read it, but two runs on the same plan can disagree, so a finding can be wrong. When you think it is, rephrase the request to be explicit about what must change and what must stay, or ask a reviewer to check it.

Where to adapt it. The prompt general/intent/rules defines the rules, each with a rule_id and a severity. Edit scope_exceeded and scope_incomplete to match how strict your organization wants to be, or add your own rules about intent in the same format; see Compliance prompts.

To enforce a convention, such as a mandatory tag, write it as a rule of the compliance policy: it then always holds, without depending on the model.

High impact (not deterministic)

What locks. While writing the report, the report model groups the changes and labels each group low, medium, or high, and the report shows the overall level as its impact banner. When the level is high and your deployment enables orchestration.block_on_high_impact, the session is locked. The banner is shown on every report either way.

Why it is not deterministic. Whether a change causes downtime or touches something critical depends on the resource, the attribute, and its context: renaming a tag and changing a firewall rule are both in-place updates in the plan. A model makes that judgment from written criteria, so the same plan can be rated differently on two runs.

Where to adapt it. The criteria are the prompt general/compliance/impact. The shipped criteria are: high for destructive actions, restarts, service downtime, or critical networking changes; medium for configuration updates that change behavior without guaranteed downtime; low for isolated new resources or non-disruptive updates such as tags or locks. Rewrite them to match your organization’s change-management standards, for example to rate any change to a production network as high; see Compliance prompts.

If a specific change must always be stopped, such as any change to a firewall, write it as a rule of the compliance policy: a high-impact verdict is a model’s judgment and is not guaranteed.

What you see

When you continue to the pull request, the Deployment Blocked screen opens instead of the merge controls; see the screenshot in Merge and apply.

  • Expand Why to see the cause: High impact with the impact summary, Compliance with each blocking finding (severity, rule id, resource, and a policy or intent badge), or both. Show all findings adds the warnings and the suggested fixes.

  • Check again reads the lock state again, for example after a reviewer unlocked the session.

  • Back to Report returns to the report. Contact Team sends a support request.

The full compliance report, including the rules that passed, is also in the session details, as the round’s Compliance Check artifact.

While locked, you can still read everything, continue the conversation, and create the pull request so reviewers can read the diff. Merge and apply are refused with 409. Your sessions list shows the lock in the Apply column.

Who is notified

When the notifications sidecar is enabled, Kumoss sends a "Compliance check failed" notification, a "High impact changes detected" notification, or both. They go to you and to every user with the panel role editor or higher, so a reviewer usually knows about the lock before you ask.

If your browser tab is hidden when the round finishes, you also get a "Session blocked" browser notification.

Unlock the session

Choose one of the two ways:

  1. Fix the request. Read the findings under Why, then send a follow-up request that addresses them, for example "keep the existing bucket and only add the tag". When the new round passes all three checks, the lock clears and the merge controls return.

  2. Ask a reviewer. A user with the panel role editor or admin reviews the findings in the admin panel and unlocks the session; see Lock or unlock the apply of a session. When they are done, choose Check again.

    Figure 1. A reviewer opens the locked session from the admin panel’s Sessions tab and presses Apply Locked to unlock it
A reviewer’s unlock lasts only until your next generate round on the session. That round runs the checks again and sets the lock afresh.

Verify

The Deployment Blocked screen no longer opens when you continue to the pull request, the Apply column of your sessions list shows the session unlocked, and Approve PR and Apply is available. Continue with Merge and apply.
